  • EstablishedPlucked strings use the Karplus–Strong digital waveguide: a delay line one period long with a loss filter, the standard physical model of a vibrating string.
  • EstablishedBowed strings (violin, cello, any zone in pizzicato or bow) use a two-part waveguide with a friction curve, after J. O. Smith's bowed-string model.
  • EstablishedWhere you pluck changes the tone: plucking near the end is bright, plucking at the middle removes the even harmonics. Harmonic mode plays real natural harmonics at ½, ⅓, ¼ and ⅕ of the string.
  • EstablishedSympathetic resonance: strings tuned to a unison, octave or fifth of the note you play start ringing on their own, as on a sitar.
  • ApproximationThe sitar and veena buzz (jawari) is modelled as a one-sided bridge barrier. Each instrument's wooden body is two resonant filters, not a full body model.
  • Sound designAmps, pedals, the orchestra layer and the cinematic layer are effects designed for this instrument, in the spirit of a plugged-in rig.
  • EstablishedWave markers show the physics of a standing wave: nodes stay still, antinodes move most, no-node strings are at rest. String motion is slowed down so you can see it.
  • TheoryThe name comes from Multiversal Codependence Theory, Mandeep S. Bhullar's own speculative framework (polygarity → qudit → qutron → multitarity), not established physics. Here, polygarity: many singularities, each fully independent, so every string here rings on its own.
